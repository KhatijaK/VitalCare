Preparing for your first infusion +

Your care team will provide instructions specific to your medication and treatment plan. Before the first appointment:

  • Confirm the date, time, location, and estimated length of the infusion.
  • Keep an updated list of medications, allergies, and relevant medical information available.
  • Ask whether you should eat, drink, or take your usual medications before treatment.
  • Wear comfortable clothing that allows the nurse to reach the infusion area easily.
  • Arrange transportation or caregiver support when recommended by your care team.
  • Have the care team’s phone number available in case your schedule or condition changes.

Always follow the instructions provided by your prescriber, pharmacist, or infusion nurse. Preparation may differ by medication.

Home infusion versus alternate-site care +

The appropriate treatment setting depends on the prescribed therapy, your clinical needs, insurance requirements, and the availability of trained support.

  • Home infusion: Therapy is coordinated in the patient’s home when the medication, home environment, and care plan are suitable.
  • Alternate-site infusion: Therapy takes place in an appropriate outpatient setting with clinical equipment and staff available on site.
  • The care team may review medication requirements, previous reactions, vascular access, mobility, caregiver support, and distance from emergency services.
  • The treatment setting can change if the patient’s condition, therapy, or coverage changes.

The prescribing provider and infusion team determine whether a treatment setting is clinically appropriate. Not every therapy is available in every setting.

Insurance and benefits coordination +

After receiving a complete referral, the intake team can begin reviewing coverage requirements for the prescribed therapy.

  • Verify the patient’s active insurance plan and demographic information.
  • Review benefit requirements, network participation, and whether prior authorization is needed.
  • Request missing clinical records or documentation required by the health plan.
  • Discuss available coverage information and potential patient responsibility when it can be determined.
  • Coordinate with the prescribing office when an authorization, appeal, or alternative documentation is needed.
  • Recheck coverage when the insurance plan or therapy changes.

Coverage verification is not a guarantee of payment. Final coverage and patient responsibility are determined by the health plan and the terms in effect when services are provided.
